Project Outline — Brand & Product Documentation
I. Core Concept & Positioning

A public leaderboard of consumer rage.

Every complaint is a Blowup — posted once, live within a minute, visible to everyone immediately. Anyone who's dealt with the same company piles on, and every pile-on adds to a running, public number called Heat.

What it is. Heat climbs a ladder that anyone can watch move in real time. Nothing is checked before it's posted, nothing is reviewed after, and nothing ever comes down. The record only ever grows, and growing is the entire point.

Category. Outrage Media — equal parts scoreboard, open mic, and permanent record. It runs on the instincts of a leaderboard game — climb the ranks, beat last week's number, earn the badge — applied to the one thing almost everyone already has an infinite supply of: a story about a company that wronged them.

Problem statement. A single frustrated customer is easy to absorb, deflect, or simply wait out. A number that's climbing in public, in real time, with no ceiling and no expiration date, is not. Most people who've been wronged by a company have nowhere to put that energy that actually does anything with it — a call center reading from a script, a review buried under ten thousand others, a tweet that gets one reply and dies by morning. Blowup exists to give that energy somewhere to go where it doesn't disappear, where it finds company fast, and where it keeps growing until it's a number a company can't pretend not to see.

Core mechanism. Post what happened. Everyone who's had it happen to them piles on. Every pile-on is public, attached to a real account, and permanent. The number climbs a ladder in front of everyone. The bigger a company's number gets, the more places it shows up — on its own site, on category leaderboards, and eventually on the one leaderboard that matters most: Most Ratio'd.

Nobody organizes a movement to send one strongly worded email. People organize because the story is better with company, the number means more in public, and a complaint a thousand people are standing behind stops being a complaint and starts being a fact everyone has to deal with. Blowup isn't built to referee two sides of a story. It's built so the side that usually loses the argument finally gets to be the loudest voice in the room.

II. Brand Architecture

The Logo

Two directions still on the table — both built on the same idea: something that reads as harmless right up until it isn't.

Option 1

The Bottle & The Bow

A champagne bottle, wrapped in a bow — except the bow is a Molotov cocktail. One half of the image is a celebration. The other half is a warning. Popped or thrown, something's about to go off. The wick is lit at the mouth of the bottle, the ribbon tails are cut to flame points — a lockup built to read as festive from ten feet away and incendiary up close, which is exactly how Blowup wants to be misjudged, right up until it isn't.

Option 2

The Countdown

A stopwatch built in the shape of a grenade, counting down instead of up. The safety lever doubles as the stem, the pull ring as the winder, the face ticks down like a fuse timer. It trades "this already went off" for "this is about to" — same warning, shorter clock.

Tagline System

Other potential taglines:

Shake it. Watch it blow. Pull the pin. Every bottle has a breaking point. Uncork the outrage. Some things aren't built to stay bottled up. Built to go off.

A champagne bottle and a Molotov cocktail run on the same physics: pressure, sealed in glass, waiting for a reason to leave. Consumer frustration behaves exactly the same way — it doesn't go away just because nobody's listening, it just gets corked. Shelved. Shaken a little harder by the next dropped call, the next canned apology, the next "we take your feedback seriously." Pressure that's never allowed to diffuse doesn't defuse itself — it just waits for a spark. Blowup is the pin, the pop, the release valve, whichever metaphor gets there first. Pull it here, loud and on purpose, or it goes off somewhere you don't get to choose.

IV. Voice, Tone & Messaging House

The friend who hypes your rant.

Not the one who tells you to calm down and put it in writing. Loud on purpose, funny when it can be, never apologetic about either.

Voice principles

Lead with the scoreboard, not the backstory. "40,000 people are behind her" does more work than a paragraph explaining what happened.

Numbers are the emotional engine. They're never rounded down for politeness and never buried in a footnote — they're set big, and they move live on the page.

The crowd is the main character. Copy talks about "the mob," "everyone," "this many people" more often than it talks about any single Handle.

Nothing gets softened. "Torch," "wreck," "bury," "expose" are the house vocabulary — a milder word would undersell what's actually happening on the page.

Humor is allowed to be a little mean. It should never be cruel toward the person posting — only toward the company on the other end of the number.

Example headline copy

14,822 people just found out they weren't the only one.

This company's Burn Ratio just did something it's never done before.

Somebody's about to hit Nuclear.

The Drop is in six hours. Get your Blowup in before the count locks.

They called it an isolated incident. 9,114 people called it Tuesday.

Just entered the Hall of Flame. Nobody's throwing them a party.

The pin's out. The Heat's climbing. There's no putting this back.

Most Ratio'd just got a new #1 — and it happened in under an hour.

V. Product Mechanism — How It Works

Starting a Blowup

Pick a company, choose a format, post. Under a minute, no structured form, no field asking what outcome you'd accept — the platform isn't in the business of negotiating on anyone's behalf.

Video Rant

Up to 90 seconds, vertical, auto-captioned on upload.

Receipts

Screenshots, order confirmations, chat logs, photos — a horizontal filmstrip under the post.

Voice Memo

For when typing feels too slow; plays back with a live waveform.

Quick Take

A plain-text post under 280 characters, for when there's no receipt, just a story.

A single Blowup can combine all four.

Piling On

One tap, under your own Handle. That tap is the entire bar — nothing about the account's history or the claim's content is checked first. The only thing that determines whether a Blowup keeps climbing is whether other people show up for it.

Burn Sites

Created automatically the instant the first Blowup names a company — nobody builds one, claims one, or waits for one to exist. A Burn Site shows the Blast Mark at its current size, the live Burn Ratio, a running Heat-over-time graph, every Blowup ever filed against that company in order, the current Boycott Pledge count, and a permanent timestamp marking the day its first Blowup went up.

Comments & the record

Every Blowup has an open comment thread. A company can post in it exactly like anyone else, under a visible account, with no separate channel and no different weight given to what it says. Nothing about a reply changes a Blowup's Heat, its tier, or its place on any leaderboard.

A Blowup can't be edited after posting beyond the original poster adding more Receipts to it. It doesn't get taken down, archived out of view, or expire on a retention schedule. Aftermath — the permanent archive — holds every Blowup ever posted, searchable, in order, permanently. It's the closest thing the platform has to institutional memory, and it's built to only ever get longer.

VI. The Gamification Layer

Handles, Clout & Torches

Every account is a persistent, pseudonymous Handle. Clout is earned by starting a Blowup that climbs, by piling on early — before a Blowup hits Trending — and by pulling in new followers. Early action is worth far more than late action: being pile-on #4 on something that eventually goes Nuclear earns vastly more Clout than being #4,000.

Rage Streaks

Consecutive days of activity — posting, piling on, or sharing — tracked with a flame icon next to a Handle's name. Missing a day resets it. No grace period.

Squads

A standing group of Handles — a friend group, a subreddit, a Discord server — who pile on together. Every Squad has its own Clout total, its own place on the Squad leaderboard, and Torches of its own to earn. Squads can be public and joinable or private and invite-only.

VII. What Blowup Optimizes For

Four numbers. One strategy.

01

Total Heat

Generated platform-wide, all-time.

02

Active Handles

Total accounts posting, piling on, or sharing.

03

Live Burn Sites

Total company pages with at least one active Blowup.

04

Boycott Pledges

Total pledges cast, platform-wide.

Attention is the currency the entire product is built to accumulate — the bigger and more permanent the record gets, the harder it becomes for any company anywhere to act like it isn't being watched. That's the whole strategy: get loud enough, for long enough, that being ignored stops being an option.

Iconography

Flame, lightning bolt, megaphone, siren, and comic-style burst shapes for tier-up moments. Nothing literal or weapon-shaped — the aggression lives in the numbers and the motion, not in the icon set.

Motion principle

Motion is constant and layered on purpose. Counters tick live wherever they appear. The screen pulses at every tier change. A shower of ember particles fires on every pile-on. A siren-style flash sweeps the page the moment something goes Nuclear. Motion isn't decoration — it's the emotional payload the product is built to deliver.

X. Culture & Rituals

The recurring moments

The Drop

Every Monday at noon, the Most Ratio'd leaderboard locks and reveals its new order, alongside "Biggest Mover of the Week" — the company whose Burn Ratio rose fastest. Built to be a recurring, screenshot-ready moment on its own.

Hall of Flame Induction

The first time a company's Burn Ratio crosses into Nuclear, its Burn Site receives a permanent induction badge, and a short animated sequence plays once, live, at the exact moment it happens — anyone on the site at the time can watch it happen in real time.

Blowup of the Year

An annual crowning of the single loudest Blowup posted that year, ranked purely on Heat rather than a vote, permanently pinned at the top of Aftermath.

Heat Index Milestones

Every time the platform-wide Heat Index crosses a round number, it's flagged on the homepage as its own small event — a number built to be dropped into a headline without needing further explanation.

XI. Go-to-Market Strategy

How it catches

Cold Start

Every company's Burn Site already exists in potential — it comes into being the moment the first Blowup names it. No onboarding step, no claim flow, no waiting period.

Creator Seeding

Recruit consumer-rage and complaint-comedy creators as the first Instigators, so the platform's earliest posts set the tone and format for everyone who follows.

Share-First Design

Every Blowup exports as a ready-to-post vertical video card the moment it's published, captions burned in, one tap from Blowup to anywhere else.

The Drop as Press Hook

Feed consumer-affairs reporters The Drop every week, pre-formatted as a ready-made story.

Community Challenges

A different industry gets spotlighted each month with a hashtag prompt; the only prizes are Torches, Burn Site placement, and Squad leaderboard position.

Squad Recruiting

Every Squad grows by pulling in more Handles — joining one is inherently an invitation to bring more people along.

XII. Phased Roadmap

Four phases

Phase 1

Ignition

Start a Blowup, Pile On, live Heat counter, auto-created Burn Sites, a basic Most Ratio'd leaderboard, and Aftermath as a flat, permanent archive.

Phase 2

Amplification

Full content formats (Video Rant, Receipts, Voice Memo, Quick Take), the complete gamification layer (Clout, Instigator Tiers, Torches, Rage Streaks), Boycott Pledge, category Halls of Flame, and The Blast Radius personalized feed.

Phase 3

Culture

Squads, The Drop as a standing weekly institution, live Hall of Flame Induction moments, the Heat Index on the homepage, and the first Blowup of the Year.

Phase 4

Ubiquity

Blowup becomes the default reference point in consumer culture — the place a Burn Ratio gets checked before a purchase, the leaderboard journalists cite without needing to explain it, and Aftermath becomes the closest thing that exists to a permanent public memory of how a company actually treated its customers.

What the outward voice doesn't say

Everything above is written for the public. This section is written for the founding team, Legal, and Trust & Safety — the operating decisions and the strategic reasoning behind them.

Moderation Floor

Legal compliance only — nothing else

No proactive review of any Blowup before or after posting. Removal is limited to a narrow legal floor: content that is illegal on its face (e.g. CSAM), non-consensual intimate imagery, doxxed personal information about a private individual unrelated to the complaint, direct and credible threats of violence, and material removed under a valid court order. Everything else — one-sided, unverified, exaggerated, or simply false — stays up by design. Company rebuttals are treated identically to any other reply: same thread, same visibility, no pinning, no verification badge.

Integrity Controls

Verify the count, never the claim

Rate limits and device/account fingerprinting slow coordinated brigading rings from faking a pile-on surge. A single Handle can only pile on a given Blowup once. Anomalous pile-on velocity is throttled and reviewed — to keep Heat numbers credible enough that press and the leaderboard hold up under scrutiny, not to determine whether the underlying complaint is true. This distinction is the entire trust model and should never be blurred in any public-facing copy.

Legal Posture

Neutral host, not publisher

Blowup is positioned publicly and contractually as a neutral host of user-submitted claims. Platform-level copy states participation counts ("14,822 people have posted about this company") rather than endorsing any claim's truth. Individual posters carry their own liability for what they post; Handle pseudonymity is a product choice, not a shield promised in the Terms of Service. All Burn Site templates, homepage copy, and push notification language route through legal review before ship to keep the platform's own voice strictly descriptive.

Crisis Escalation

Fixed ladder, fixed SLA

Cease-and-desist letters, subpoenas, and non-emergency legal threats route to Trust & Safety lead → Legal → Comms on a fixed SLA. Anything involving a credible safety threat bypasses that queue entirely and escalates directly.

The Long Game — need to know

Blowup is not the fix. It's the pressure.

Blowup is built to be loud, one-sided, and permanent on purpose. That discomfort isn't a side effect to manage down over time — it's the mechanism. A public number that only grows, with no appeals process and no company able to argue its way off the leaderboard, creates exactly the kind of pressure that makes a calmer, structured, dispute-resolution product look like relief by comparison once it exists.

Blowup isn't designed to solve the problem it surfaces. It's designed to make the problem loud enough, for long enough, that solving it stops being optional — for whoever builds that next, quieter product. This rationale is internal framing only. It does not appear in public-facing material, consumer press, investor materials aimed outside the company, or any company-facing communication.
