How It Moves

Volume decides visibility. It never decides the outcome.

Every mechanic is built to answer one question honestly: could this be mistaken for a pile-on? The answer is always no — because escalation is driven by unresponsiveness and severity, not noise alone.

01

Search first, always

We match your issue against existing cases before you file anything. Most problems already have a case — you just add your voice to it.

02

Add Your Case, or file new

Joining verifies you as a second claimant on the same real issue — never an anonymous upvote. No match found means a short, structured filing instead.

03

Escalation, on a clock

A category-specific SLA starts the moment a company is notified. Cases move up the ladder when backers cross a threshold or the clock lapses — whichever comes first.

04

Resolved means resolved

Only you — the filer — can close your case. A company can mark "Response Sent," but that's not the same thing, and everyone can tell the difference.

The Escalation Ladder

Every case moves through the same five stages, in public.

  1. Filed

    0 hrs

    The company is notified. A 48-hour acknowledgment clock starts immediately.

  2. Under Review

    Acknowledged

    The standard SLA clock starts — 5 business days for retail, 10 for financial services, matched to what's realistic for the industry.

  3. Building Support

    Threshold or lapse

    Triggered automatically once verified backers cross a category threshold, or the SLA clock runs out — whichever happens first.

  4. Escalated

    Senior queue

    Routed into the company's senior support queue and shown as an open, aging case on their public profile.

  5. Resolved

    Filer-confirmed

    Published permanently to the company's case history. Resolved and unresolved cases both stay visible — that's what makes the record mean something.

Built To Survive Scrutiny

Protective, not aggressive. Verified, not viral.

Verified, never named

You verify with an email and optional proof of purchase. The public case page never shows your name — the company sees a case reference, not your identity.

Right of reply, always
