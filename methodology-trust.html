Identity Model

Verified, but never named.

You confirm who you are so your case counts. The company only ever sees proof that a real person filed it — never who that person is, unless you choose to reveal it for direct contact.

What we verify at filing

  • A confirmed email address
  • Optional proof of relationship — order number, account reference, or receipt
  • A minimum account age before a case counts toward escalation
  • Device and account fingerprinting to prevent duplicate claims

What's never shown on the public case page

  • Your name
  • Your email address
  • Your account or order number
  • Any personally identifying detail, ever

Built To Survive Scrutiny

Volume decides visibility. It never decides the outcome.

Every escalation mechanic is designed so a case can't be inflated by noise, and a company can't stall a real one out.

Rate limiting & fingerprinting

"Add Your Case" is rate-limited and fingerprinted at the device and account level, so a case can't be inflated by mass account creation in a single day.

A minimum verification bar

An account has to be a set number of days old and pass basic verification before it counts toward an escalation threshold at all.

Escalation needs unresponsiveness, too

Cases move up the ladder when backers cross a threshold or the SLA clock lapses — whichever comes first — so a quiet company can't just wait out a real problem.

Every case is disputable

A company can flag any case as inaccurate for moderator review at any time. Volume never overrides that right.

The Escalation Ladder

Every case moves through the same five stages, in public.

  1. Filed

    0 hrs

    The company is notified. A 48-hour acknowledgment clock starts immediately.

  2. Under Review

    Acknowledged

    The standard SLA clock starts, sized to what's realistic for the industry — see the table below.

  3. Building Support

    Threshold or lapse

    Triggered automatically once verified backers cross a category threshold, or the SLA clock runs out — whichever happens first.

  4. Escalated

    Senior queue

    Routed into the company's senior support queue and shown as an open, aging case on their public profile.

  5. Resolved

    Filer-confirmed

    Published permanently to the company's case history. Resolved and unresolved cases both stay visible.

Right Of Reply & Dispute

The legal safety valve, made visible.

Every case carries a public company-response thread, and a formal appeal path for anything a company believes is fabricated or inaccurate.

01

Respond or flag

A company can publicly respond to any case at any time, or formally flag it as inaccurate for review.

02

Moderator review

A named moderation team reviews flagged cases against our published moderation policy — never just one side's word.

03

Evidence requested

Both the filer and the company get a chance to submit evidence before a case's status or accuracy is affected.

04

Decision published

The outcome is published permanently alongside the case, whichever way it goes — that's what keeps the record credible.

Regulatory Bridge

We route legitimate cases faster. We don't route around anyone.

Once a case is severity-flagged and aging — financial harm, safety, fraud — Regulatory Bridge can forward its evidence, with one click, to the regulator that actually has authority. Uprush is complementary to that authority, never a substitute or a quasi-legal one.

Data Privacy & Retention

Your evidence is handled like the sensitive data it often is.

What you upload

Receipts, screenshots, and correspondence are optional and attached only to your case — never displayed publicly, only referenced by the company as case evidence.

How long we keep it

Evidence is retained for the life of the case, then deleted on a published schedule — unless it's part of an active dispute or a regulatory referral you initiated.

Sensitive categories

Financial, insurance, and health-adjacent cases get additional handling — matching the disclosure and retention expectations those categories already carry under existing regulation.
